## Why this is the interesting gap, not a boring one

**Selecting `VK_PRESENT_MODE_FIFO_KHR` gives you vsync. It does not give you
pacing.** The difference decides how a handheld feels:

- A frame that takes 20 ms at 60 Hz misses its vsync and waits for the next one.
  **The effective rate drops to 30, and worse, it alternates** — 16.6, 33.3,
  16.6, 33.3. That alternation is judder, and it is more visible than a stable
  30.
- **Swappy exists to pick a stable divisor and hold it**, pacing evenly at 30
  rather than thrashing between 60 and 30, and to negotiate the display refresh
  rate rather than assuming it.

**That is exactly the emulator case on this device.** An emulator frequently
cannot hold 60, the panels do 120 but are **currently capped to 60 by a user
setting**, and the whole product promise is that a game feels playable.

**Two forks reached half the idea independently.** azahar and melonDS use
`Choreographer`, which is the vsync signal but not the pacing policy. And
**ARMSX2 wrote a `FrameGenPacer`** for its frame-generation subsystem — a
separate pacer, because generated frames need their own pacing decision. **It
discovered that pacing needs an owner, and solved it only for generated
frames.**

## The inverted parallel with audio

Surveyed an hour earlier: **three forks independently adopted Oboe**, Google's
recommended low-latency audio library, without coordinating.

**On frames, the equivalent library exists and not one fork adopted it.**

| | Audio | Frames |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google's library | Oboe | **Swappy** |
| Forks using it | **3** | **0** |
| Fleet state | converged | **nothing** |

Same platform, same vendor, same kind of library, opposite outcome. **Audio
latency is audible immediately and frame pacing is not**, which is the most
likely explanation and is a guess, not a finding.

## What follows

1. **Frame pacing is a shared-layer subsystem with no incumbent.** Unlike every
   other candidate, there is nothing to extract, nothing to reconcile and no
   licence question. It is the cheapest thing in the queue to own.
2. **It belongs with the presenter**, which is already the largest open question
   in the device-layer extraction: `vulkan_presenter.cc` is 3,879 lines and owns
   the swapchain, and the two-guest-screen question lands there too.
3. **Check the 60 Hz cap before measuring anything.** A pacing result taken
   under `PRIORITY_USER_SETTING_PEAK_REFRESH_RATE` at 60 measures the setting,
   not the hardware.
4. **ARMSX2's `FrameGenPacer` is prior art for the policy**, even though it is
   not a general pacer. Read it before designing one.

## Not claimed

**No pacing was measured.** This says which library each fork calls, nothing
about frame times. Whether Swappy beats plain FIFO on this device, and whether
it fights ADPF or the 60 Hz cap, are experiments and belong in the ledger.
